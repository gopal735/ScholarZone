## Verdict

`needs_review`. I read the awarding body's own pages in full and the programme is
real, current and correctly described: the body, the deadline wording, the
eligibility criteria and the funding amount all agree with the catalogue record.
Two things nevertheless stop this being `active`.

1. **No cycle is open today.** The application window runs "10 April to
   30 September" every year and the 2026 window closed on 30 September 2026,
   twelve days before this check. The programme is between cycles, so the record
   cannot be shown to describe a currently-open cycle.
2. **The page publishes no degree level.** The award is a 3–12 month research or
   training exchange, not a degree, so the record's `Postdoctoral` label is an
   editorial inference rather than something the source states.

This is not `conflicting`: nothing on the pages contradicts the record. It is not
`quarantined`: the official page exists and was read.

## Quotes that carry the verdict

**Awarding body — confirmed.**

> The HFA provides grants up to €10,000 to help young members undertake
> specialised heart failure research or training in an ESC member country
> outside their own country. Application period is 10 April to 30 September.

The page sits under Education > Career Development > Grants on escardio.org, and
the ESC's own navigation lists "HFA: Heart failure" as one of its associations.

**Deadline — confirmed as stated, but the cycle is closed.**

> The online application form must be submitted between 10 April and 30
> September each year.

The conditions page also gives the timeline: "10 April - 30 September" is the
"Call for applications", "December" is the "Results announcement", and "May Y+1"
is the "Deadline to start the accepted exchange period". No year is attached to
the window anywhere on the page, so no exact calendar date can be quoted; the
next window would be expected to open on 10 April 2027, but that is my inference
from "each year", not a published date.

**Eligibility — confirmed, with one gap in the record's summary.**

> Applicants should fulfil the following conditions as of the application
> deadline:
> 2.1 Be under 40 years old at the day of application deadline
> 2.2 Be an HFA Member, or Fellow of the HFA
> 2.3 Hold a MD but have not yet obtained a post defined as 'permanent', 'senior
> staff' or 'consultant' and be able to demonstrate expertise in Heart Failure
> through examination, practice, scientific publication in a relevant journal or
> abstract presentation at a national or international meeting; OR
> 2.4 Hold a PhD, DPhil degree or equivalent, or have submitted a thesis before
> starting the research period relating to the grant.

Combined with "The project proposal must be prepared in collaboration with the
proposed host centre for a period of minimum three months" and the requirement to
work "in an ESC member country outside their own country", every distinct claim in
the record's eligibility summary is confirmed.

The record's summary, however, omits a selection criterion that materially
narrows the field:

> Citizenship or permanent residence in a low/middle-income country as defined by
> the World Bank – 20%

It is weighted at 20% of the grade, alongside feasibility and importance at 50%.

**Funding — confirmed as partially funded.**

> HFA Grant recipients will receive €2,500 per month with a maximum of €10,000
> according to the length of the project.

> The ESC will not provide assistance with transfer or household removal expenses
> incurred by grant recipients on taking up an award.

So the record's `Partially Funded` is right. This is a stipend for a research or
training exchange; it is not tuition support and not travel support.

**Degree levels — unconfirmed.**

> The HFA Grants programme enable medical and science graduates, who have
> completed the major part of their cardiology training but have not yet obtained
> a permanent status, to undertake specialised heart failure research or training
> in an ESC Member Country, outside their own country.

(The ESC's own sentence contains the grammatical slip "enable"; it is quoted
verbatim, and an inline link was removed from the middle of the sentence.) The
project "may be undertaken for any period from three to 12 months" and is
"normally be administered through the medium of a fixed-term contract of
employment". The page never states a degree level and no degree is conferred.
"Postdoctoral" is a defensible description of the target audience but it is not a
published claim.

## What a person should know before trusting the record

- **Do not show this record as currently open.** The 2026 window closed on
  30 September 2026. Re-check from 10 April 2027, when the next annual window
  should open.
- **The eligibility summary is incomplete.** The World Bank low/middle-income
  country criterion carries 20% of the grade. Without it, the record implies
  eligibility to applicants who will not realistically be selected.
- **The record's country is wrong.** It reads Switzerland. The ESC's offices page
  states: "We have two administrative centres at the ESC: the European Heart House
  in France and the Brussels Office in Belgium." No Swiss office is listed.
  "Multiple" or "Europe" would be more accurate. This is reported for the reviewer
  because country is not one of the five checks this campaign requires, and I did
  not let it drive the verdict.
- **Everything else holds.** Awarding body, funding, eligibility criteria and the
  annual deadline wording were all read directly from the awarding body's own
  pages and all agree with the record.

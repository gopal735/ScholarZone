## Claim-by-claim

**Awarding body — confirmed.** The page is on the awarding organisation's own domain and names both bodies:

> "The United Nations University World Institute for Development Economics Research (UNU-WIDER), together with the National Treasury of South Africa (NT), is requesting research proposals under the third phase of the Southern Africa – Towards Inclusive Economic Development (SA-TIED) programme."

**Deadline — confirmed.** An exact date is published, and it is a currently open cycle:

> "Closing date: 15 October 2026, 23:59 (UTC+2)"

The milestone table repeats it as "15 October 2026 proposal submission deadline", and the submission form says "Deadline for applications is 15 October 2026 23:59 UTC+2." The questions deadline of 8 October 2026 has already passed. **Three days remain on this cycle.**

**Eligibility — confirmed.** Every distinct claim in the record is stated on the page, near-verbatim:

> "This call invites South Africa-based and international researchers to submit proposals to conduct research primarily, but not exclusively, using the South African tax administrative data."

> "Proposals from individuals, groups of individuals, and non-profit organizations are welcome. Applications from women and South African researchers are particularly encouraged. Special consideration will be given to research teams that include junior and early-career researchers as well as scholars from the Global South."

> "Preference will be given to applications from early-career and female scholars, as well as researchers from South Africa."

**Funding — confirmed.** The record's `Research Funding` type is right, and it is *not* a fully funded award:

> "A total research honorarium in the range of US$3,000–10,000 will be paid upon delivery of a working paper, depending on the qualifications of the lead researcher and assessment of the proposal."

The honorarium is expected to cover all research expenses, with an additional small travel stipend for researchers using the South African tax administrative data who live outside Gauteng. No tuition, fees or living costs are paid.

**Degree levels — NOT confirmed.** This is the one open item, and the sole reason the verdict is not `active`. Neither the announcement page nor the full PDF states any degree level or degree-awarding structure: this is a research grant aimed at "qualified researchers", not a study programme. More significantly, the live submission form's applicant-category selector offers:

> "I am a Professional Student Early career researcher (awarded PhD in the last 5 years)"

That "Student" option sits alongside "Professional" and "Early career researcher", so the record's `Postgraduate` degree level is not something the awarding body says — and the form's student category cuts against a postgraduate-only reading.

## What a person should know before trusting this record

1. **The record is substantially accurate.** Body, deadline, deadline date, eligibility wording and the funding nature all agree with the awarding body's own page. The programme is real, live and open *today*.
2. **It expires on 15 October 2026.** Whatever status is applied, the record needs an automatic flip to closed/expired after 23:59 UTC+2 on that date. SA-TIED will likely re-issue this RfRP for a later cycle, at which point the deadline fields must be re-checked.
3. **The degree-level label is an editorial inference.** The catalogue says "Postgraduate"; the official pages say nothing of the kind. Recommend changing it to a value that reflects a non-degree research grant (for example "Not a degree programme – research grant for qualified researchers"), or marking it unspecified.
4. **Funding is modest and deliverable-linked.** US$3,000–10,000 is an honorarium paid only on delivery of an accepted working paper, not a scholarship covering study costs — the card should not imply "fully funded".
5. **It is not a scholarship for students to apply for in the usual sense.** The applicant signs a consultant contract with UNU-WIDER and transfers copyright of the resulting paper. That is a material condition a prospective applicant should see before applying.

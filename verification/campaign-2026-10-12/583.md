## Eligibility — the record's own claims confirmed, but the summary is incomplete

Every distinct claim in the record's `eligibility_summary` is confirmed verbatim, on both tracks.

> "Journalists must be able to demonstrate relevant experience of working as an employed or freelance journalist as their main occupation and provide appropriate samples of their published or broadcast work. They must demonstrate to the Advisory Board's satisfaction a genuine desire to broaden and deepen their knowledge of science, rather than seek support for immediate publication opportunities."

> "Life scientists seeking support to transition into full time (science) journalism must have a master's degree or higher, and preferably in a life sciences discipline."

> "Personal blog posts or social media postings, or publication in academic journals of commentary or reviews of research, are not considered sufficient evidence of prior journalism."

What the record's summary leaves out is the rule that screens most applicants:

> "Applicants must reside in an EMBC Member State, and their placement/s must also occur in an EMBC Member State. The placements must be in-person to maximize opportunities for learning and engagement. Virtual placements are not eligible."

> "If Fellows wish to undertake two or more placements at least one must be in a different EMBC Member State than the other/s. Applicants are not subject to any age restriction."

"EMBC Member State" is a specific list of 32 countries (Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Montenegro, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovak Republic, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Türkiye, United Kingdom) per https://www.embo.org/about-embo/embc/. Also from the Eligibility tab: Fellows are expected not to undertake other work during the fellowship, and journalists must not report on their host research institution during the term.

## Funding — confirmed as a stipend

`funding_type: Stipend` matches. It is a living-cost stipend, not a tuition award — no tuition or fee waiver appears anywhere on the pages.

> "Successful applicants receive a daily stipend based on the cost of living in the country of their placement/s. The stipends are intended to cover living expenses and travel costs. Fellows are responsible for their own social security contributions, taxes or other costs incurred."

Durations, both tracks:

> "For journalists, the duration of the fellowship is for a minimum of three months and a maximum of six months. Journalist Fellows will also be expected to attend a two-day workshop at EMBO in Heidelberg, Germany for which travel and accommodation will be provided."

> "For researchers wishing to become science journalists the duration of the fellowship is for a minimum of three months and a maximum of 12 months. Periods longer than three months will be subject to an ongoing assessment process."

## What a person should know before trusting this record

1. **The deadline and the funding type are solid.** `30 October, 23:59 CEST`, annual, applications accepted at any time before then, stipend for living expenses and travel. Those fields are safe to trust as of 12 October 2026.
2. **Do not read `country: Multiple` as "anywhere in the world".** Residence and the placement must both be inside an EMBC Member State. A candidate outside those 32 countries is not eligible regardless of anything else on the card.
3. **`degree_levels: Postgraduate, Professional` is not evidenced.** EMBO's pages state no level offered. Treat this field as unreconciled, not as confirmed.
4. **No stipend amount is published in a form I could read.** Do not quote a daily or monthly figure for this record from this check.
5. **This is a sabbatical/internship fellowship, not a study award.** It suits a working journalist taking funded time out at a research institute, or a scientist moving into a newsroom. It is not a route to a degree and confers no qualification.
